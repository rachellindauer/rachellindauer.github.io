Memories

Why

Snapchat caps how many saved memories you get to keep and charges for anything past the line. Ten years of mine are in there, 2016 to now, and I did not have a copy of a single one of them anywhere else.

What

The export does not hand back a photo library. It hands back parts. Every memory comes out split in two, the picture or video in one file and the caption you wrote on it in a separate see-through image, so they arrive as strangers who have to be reintroduced. Most of mine turned out to have no caption at all.

So the work is pairing every caption back to the photo it belongs to, painting it back on, and ending up with something that behaves like a real library instead of a folder holding 2,659 loose pieces.

Log

  • Sep 9

    Started the public version, and it told me I had the wrong number written down since August. I had 2,007 recorded as too low. It was right. The manifest says 2,063 because it counts saved copies rather than memories, and 56 of mine were saved twice. Take off the 16 videos Snapchat never delivered and you get the 1,991 I actually received.

    The audit reads 356 KB of 5.02 GB, because a zip keeps its list of contents at the end of the file rather than the start.

    My duplicate checker was wrong three times and Apple Photos is what caught it. I stopped reasoning about the zip and hashed all 1,991 finished files instead. 1,972 distinct pictures.

    The library is 1,972 now and in Photos, every date checked. There is a calendar file too, one day for each of the 341 days I have memories on.

  • Aug 27

    The library was built on the 24th, and tonight I realized I could not explain one thing that had happened. Claude built it, caught every trap, and I said “Great, let’s fix it.” That is the exact failure I know in myself, so I made myself learn the three things worth knowing.

    1. Phone videos are stored sideways, with a note saying to rotate them on playback. My captions were made for the upright version, so sizing them to the raw landscape measurements would have squashed them on 80% of my videos, and no error would have fired, because a mangled caption is still a valid video.

    2. Snapchat’s dates inside my videos are the date the servers reprocessed them, not the date I filmed. Three videos from different days in fall 2017 all claimed the same moment in January 2018. The real dates came from the zip timestamps and Snapchat’s manifest, which agreed across all 2,047 files.

    3. A script finishing with no errors means almost nothing. A clean run will happily hand you squashed captions, silent audio and wrong dates. The only checks that count compare the output against something the script never touched.

  • Aug 24

    The archives came the same day I asked, three zips, 4.7 GB. I checked what was inside before unzipping anything, which caught a count that would have hidden 16 missing videos. Everything is out. The actual work starts next.
