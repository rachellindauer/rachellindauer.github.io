VJing

Why

My fiancé DJs in his free time, and I thought learning the visual art of live musical graphics would be a cool way for us to bond over his interests.

What

Learning to write GLSL shaders, the little GPU programs behind most real-time visual effects, using Shadertoy as the sandbox. The long-term rig is TouchDesigner synced to his Ableton, so the visuals lock to his BPM live.

The style I’m chasing is my own: monochromatic, organic, growing textural fields that breathe with the music. I have a fine-arts background, and my hand-drawn work explored dense fields of thousands of tiny packed cells; this is that drawing, learning to move. Deliberately apart from the neon-kaleidoscope mainstream of festival visuals. Texture over symmetry, restraint over spectacle.

Pieces

Face field

A field of cells that gathers into my own face and comes apart again, built so that no line in it can ever be curved.

Versions

These all run right here in your browser, which still feels slightly unreal to me. I’m keeping every version so I can look back at where something started and see how far it actually got. Fair warning, they ask a lot of your laptop, so if one gets choppy just hit the left bracket key and it will ease off.

  • V3 Aug 24, off the beat

    The cells had been landing at perfectly even spacing, which read like a grid and gave the whole trick away. Nudging each one off the beat broke that up, and because of the way the walls are built it did not bend a single line.

  • V2 Aug 24, never stops moving

    No more holding still and no more waiting around. The lines are always travelling, either forming the face or coming apart again, and the face only exists for an instant at the turnaround. It moves about three times slower than V1.

  • V1 Aug 24, the first time it moved

    The first version where the field really travelled. It gathers into the face, holds it, then melts away. Watching it back, nearly all of the movement happens inside about one second and then it sits frozen, which is exactly the thing I asked to fix next.

Log

  • Aug 24

    The face moves now. Every cell grows out from a single point, and the wall you see is the exact halfway line between two neighbouring points, which is the reason nothing in this can ever curve. I gave every point a second home, so the whole field travels back and forth between an even scatter and the face, and the face only exists for an instant at the turnaround. Making that feel alive took three goes. My first version happened in a burst and then sat frozen, because I had smoothed the timing twice over without noticing, once across the whole field and again inside every single point. The fix was letting each point take its own slow trip and ordering the queue by distance out from the middle of the face, so the thing creeps outward with a ragged edge instead of arriving all at once. After that I broke up the last of the machine-made look, since the cells had been landing at perfectly even spacing and reading like a grid. The eyes are now the background of my homepage, gathering and dissolving behind the words while you read.

  • Aug 23

    Spent a long session chasing the face effect. The field stays pure abstraction most of the time, then the cells flash together into a face and drift back apart. I took every wrong turn before it worked. Shading, density, and engraving tricks all got thrown out. What finally unlocked it was realizing that nothing should ever fill in, every line keeps the exact same weight, and the face lives entirely in where the cell walls sit. We traced my features from a photo and placed cell seeds in pairs across each line so the wall between them lands right on the feature. The still frame is really close now. Next up is making the cells physically travel between a wandering home and their face position, so the geometry itself performs.

  • Jun 7

    Locked a creative target I keep coming back to: a highly detailed face rendered entirely in lines, where line density does all the shading. Learned it can’t come from procedural noise alone; it needs a real face driving where the lines pack.
